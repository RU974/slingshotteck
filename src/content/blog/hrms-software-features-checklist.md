An **HRMS (human resource management system)** brings employee data, attendance, leave, payroll and performance into one place. Pick the wrong one and HR still ends up exporting spreadsheets every month. Pick the right one and payroll that used to take days takes hours.

This checklist is based on what we see when rolling out [Slingshotteck HRMS](/hrms) for companies of different sizes: the features that matter in daily use, not just on a brochure.

## Core HR

### 1. Central employee records
Every employee's profile, contract, job history, salary, bank details and documents in one secure, searchable record, with expiry reminders for visas, contracts and certifications.

### 2. Organisation structure and roles
Departments, branches, designations and reporting lines so approvals go to the right manager automatically.

### 3. Self-service portal
Employees should be able to view payslips, apply for leave and update personal details themselves. Every self-service request is one less email to HR.

## Time and attendance

### 4. Biometric attendance integration
If your staff clock in with fingerprint or face devices, the HRMS must pull those punches automatically. Manual CSV uploads lead to errors and disputes. Check which devices are supported and how often data syncs.

### 5. Shifts, rosters and overtime
Night shifts, rotating rosters and overtime rules are where generic tools break down. Make sure the system handles shifts that cross midnight and calculates late arrivals and overtime correctly.

### 6. Leave management
Configurable leave types, accrual rules, carry-forward, public holiday calendars per location and a simple approval flow.

## Payroll

### 7. Automated payroll runs
Payroll should be calculated from attendance, leave, allowances, bonuses, loans and deductions, then produce payslips and bank transfer files with minimal manual work.

### 8. Local tax and compliance settings
Tax slabs, social security contributions and statutory deductions differ by country. Confirm the HRMS can be configured for every country you employ people in.

## Talent

### 9. Recruitment and onboarding
Job openings, candidate tracking, interview scheduling and onboarding checklists that turn a hired candidate into an employee record without retyping.

### 10. Performance reviews
Goals, review cycles and feedback stored alongside the employee record, not in separate documents.

## Platform

### 11. Reporting and dashboards
Headcount, attendance rate, turnover, overtime and payroll cost reports on demand, plus exports for finance.

### 12. Security, access control and integrations
Role-based permissions, audit logs, encryption, regular backups and APIs to connect accounting, CRM and other systems. If a vendor cannot explain how your data is protected, keep looking.

## Quick comparison table

| Area | Must have | Nice to have |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core HR | Employee records, documents, org chart | E-signatures |
| Attendance | Biometric sync, shifts, leave | Geo-fenced mobile check-in |
| Payroll | Automated runs, payslips, bank files | Loans and advances |
| Talent | Recruitment, onboarding | 360-degree reviews |
| Platform | Roles, audit log, backups, API | SSO |
